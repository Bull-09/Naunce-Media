UGC ads vs studio ads: which works better on Meta?

By the Nuance Media teamUpdated 18 November 2026

Short answer

UGC ads vs studio ads: which works better on Meta?

UGC ads usually perform better with cold audiences because they look like normal posts and earn attention fast. Polished studio ads earn their place for premium products, brand trust and retargeting. Most brands should run both and let results decide.

“Should we shoot UGC or a proper ad?” Our answer is usually “yes”. Each does a different job.

What UGC does well

  • It looks native. A creator talking to camera looks like any other post, so people watch instead of scrolling past.
  • It shows the product in real life. Unboxing, routines, problem-and-fix: proof that’s hard to fake in a studio.
  • It’s fast to test. One shoot can produce several hooks, so you learn which opener works in days.

What studio and lifestyle ads do well

  • Premium products. Jewellery, fragrance and fashion often need the product to look as good as it costs.
  • Retargeting and trust. People who already know you respond well to a polished brand moment.
  • Longevity. A strong brand film can run for longer than a single hook.

What makes a UGC ad actually sell

  1. The first three seconds. Start with the problem or the payoff, never the logo.
  2. One idea per video. Problem, proof, offer: in that order.
  3. Captions for sound-off. Plenty of people scroll with sound off.
  4. Several hooks per concept. Same video, different openers. Let Meta pick.
  5. The right creator. Someone who looks like your buyer beats someone with a big following.

Quick answers.

What is a UGC ad?

A short video ad where a real person uses or talks about a product on camera, styled like a normal social post rather than a produced commercial.

Are UGC ads better than studio ads?

For cold audiences on Meta they often are, because they look native and hold attention. Studio and lifestyle ads do better for premium products and retargeting. Test both.

How long should a UGC ad be?

Long enough to hook, show the problem and the proof, and make the offer. The first three seconds matter far more than the total length.

How many hooks should I test?

Several per concept. Shooting different openers in one session is the cheapest way to find what stops the scroll.
